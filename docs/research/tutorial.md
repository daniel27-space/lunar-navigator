# First-run tour: what it borrows and from where

Written 15 Nov 2026 after a first-time tester took too long to find the controls (he did not
discover the scroll wheel). Sources were read as search results and summaries, not in full.

| Borrowed | From | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Each step is finished by doing the action on the live scene, never by pressing Next | Super Mario Bros. 1-1, Breath of the Wild's Great Plateau, Portal | Doing builds the habit; reading does not |
| One control per step, in the order they are needed | Portal developer commentary | Early builds let players get through without understanding |
| No deck of cards before the app | Nielsen Norman Group, mobile tutorials study (70 users, 4 apps): people who skipped the deck did slightly better | Cards describe controls the user has not touched yet |
| A hint only when the user is stuck (after 8 s) | Nielsen Norman Group, "pull" help beats "push" | Help when it is wanted |
| Skippable at every point, replay from Help | Same | Professionals should not be held up |
| Actions already done count | Our judgement | Someone who zooms before being asked has shown they can |
| A tick and one fact about the Moon per step | Duolingo (value first) | The reward is learning something real |
| No text walls over a paused scene | Criticism of Kerbal Space Program's tutorials | Players read pages and still could not fly |

Evidence that a tour is worth having here at all: Andersen et al., CHI 2012 (45,000 players):
tutorials helped only in the complex game. A camera with turn, zoom and slide is complex enough.
